# Code of Conduct

Basalt's issue tracker and pull requests are working spaces. People should be able to
report a bug, question an implementation, or review a patch without being attacked
or dragged into an unrelated argument.

## Expected conduct

- Discuss the code, behavior, and evidence rather than the person presenting them.
- Give criticism directly and specifically enough that someone can act on it.
- Ask for clarification before assuming negligence or bad intent.
- Respect a clear no. Contributors and maintainers do not owe anyone a feature,
  review, deadline, or private support.
- Keep issues and pull requests on topic. Move broader design questions to a separate
  discussion when asked.
- Credit other people's work and disclose copied or generated material when its
  origin matters to licensing or review.

Harassment, threats, slurs, sexualized attention, deliberate misgendering, doxxing,
impersonation, sustained personal attacks, and publishing private communications
without permission are not accepted. Neither is repeatedly reopening a settled
argument across issues or contacting contributors elsewhere to evade moderation.

Disagreement is not misconduct. Maintainers may reject an idea, request substantial
changes, or close work that does not fit the project. Contributors may challenge
those decisions with technical evidence. The line is crossed when the discussion
turns into abuse, intimidation, or disruption.

## Scope

This policy applies to the Basalt repository, issue tracker, pull requests, release
discussions, and project-run community spaces. It also applies when someone is
representing the project elsewhere, such as speaking through an official account.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report conduct problems privately to `basaltlauncher@gmail.com` with **Basalt conduct**
in the subject. Include links or screenshots and enough context to understand what
happened. Do not use a public issue to report harassment or expose private details.

Maintainers will review the available evidence and avoid sharing the reporter's
identity beyond what is necessary to handle the report. Depending on severity and
history, action may include editing or removing content, a warning, restricting
participation, or a permanent ban. Immediate restrictions may be applied when needed
to stop ongoing harm or disruption.

Anyone handling a report in which they are directly involved must step back from the
decision when another maintainer is available.
